# Budgets, monthly summary and recommendations

## Setting a starting budget

1. Estimate current monthly spending on groceries plus household essentials from a few months of receipts or card statements. Remove clearly non-essential purchases.
2. Compare with the USDA food plans (Thrifty, Low-Cost, Moderate, Liberal), updated monthly by household member age (start at https://www.fna.usda.gov/research/cnpp/usda-food-plans, which links to the monthly cost-of-food reports). Adjust for household size and for high-cost areas (often +10% or more), then add household essentials.
3. Set a first target between current spending and the Moderate plan, then step it down after two or three months if the family keeps up. Expect costs to rise as children reach their teens.

Agree on separate holiday and hosting lines in advance for months like November and December.

## Monthly summary (one page)

Produce it on the first weekend of the month, in this order:

1. **Headline**: spending vs budget for groceries plus household essentials, with the holiday line if active.
2. **Split**: groceries, household essentials, non-essential, each by store.
3. **Trips**: planned shops per week and midweek exceptions (count and what they were for).
4. **Online orders**: order count and the share that were household items available on the warehouse-club list.
5. **Snacks**: snack share of grocery spending, the weekly trend, and the bulk vs single-serve split.
6. **Prices**: the three biggest price changes from the price book.
7. **Meals**: cooked, skipped and tossed counts; any meal tossed twice.
8. **Receipts captured**: trips with a receipt vs trips known from other evidence.
9. **One recommendation**, if the evidence supports one (below).

Keep it to one screen on a phone. Numbers with units, no filler.

## Making a recommendation

Rules:

1. **Wait for evidence.** About four weeks of data for the signal in question. Before that, report the numbers without recommending.
2. **Show the evidence.** Name the weeks, the numbers and the comparison ("Snacks were 18% of groceries in weeks 1 to 4, 70% of it single-serve packs").
3. **Make it specific and small.** One change, one month ("Switch lunchbox crackers to the warehouse-club box and portion them at home; expected saving about $25 a month").
4. **One per month** unless asked for more.
5. **The family decides.** Record the recommendation, their answer (accept, reject, tweak) and the follow-up result in `decisions.md`. Next month, report whether it worked.

Signals worth watching:

| Signal | Possible finding |
| --- | --- |
| Snack share | Single-serve packs at a high-price store |
| Tossed meals | Portions too large for one meal |
| Store drift | Small top-up trips at a conventional supermarket |
| Price creep | A staple is now cheaper at another core store |
| Online orders | Household items ordered online that are cheaper at the warehouse club |
| Midweek trips | The same item runs out every week; buy more of it on shopping day |

## Improving the process

If a step is being skipped (receipts, cooked checks), propose making it easier rather than adding reminders. Lessons that would help any family can be collected into a change list for the skill's maintainer; never include family data in it.
